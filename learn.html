Understanding oil exposure

Short explainers behind the numbers this simulator shows. Read them alongside the demo, not instead of it.

What is a barrel?

A barrel (bbl) is 42 US gallons, roughly 159 litres. Crude benchmarks are quoted in US dollars per barrel, and different grades trade at different prices because of quality and transport.

Exposure is not ownership

This demo tracks a price 1:1 — call it "1.0x exposure". You do not own barrels here; the position value simply moves in proportion to the benchmark you are tracking.

Why the entry price matters

Your profit or loss is (current price ÷ entry price − 1) × amount. Two people in the same package can see different returns purely because they started at different prices.

Volatility cuts both ways

Crude oil has historically moved one to three percent on ordinary days and far more during shocks. A scenario showing +10% deserves to be read next to the −10% case, not on its own.

Currency effects

Oil is priced in dollars, so a rupee-denominated position is also affected by USD/INR moves. This demo holds that rate fixed for simplicity, which real investing never does.

Paper trading vs real markets

Real trading involves spreads, brokerage, slippage, margin requirements, taxes and counterparty risk. None of those exist in this simulator, so real outcomes will differ — often substantially.

The four-step flow this demo follows

Same four steps on every package page.

1

Entry

A starting demo amount and a simulated oil price.

2

Price moves

A daily movement you choose or a fixed schedule you set.

3

Value changes

Amount × the movement, computed in the browser.

4

P&L

New value minus the starting amount. A hypothetical figure only.
